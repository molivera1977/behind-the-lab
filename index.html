📘 Behind the LAB

How Mr. Olivera's Lab works — every strand, every lesson, and why each one is there.

What the Lab is

The Lab is a set of short lessons on a Chromebook, each one made for the child who takes it. It sits beside the regular Grade 4 lessons, never in place of them: Lab students still do every class module with the class.

It exists because of the numbers. On the Grade 3 SBAC (Spring 2026), 17 of 22 students in this class were at Level 1 in ELA and 12 of 22 in math. This fall's interim tests showed the same gaps, skill by skill.

Who it is for, and when

Every child gets the strands that match their own results: the basics for the students furthest behind, stretch work for the middle and top. Kids work in the Lab during specials and WIN time, and the teacher circulates.

How every lesson works

Every lesson has the same five parts. Click a part to see what happens in it.

Made for each child

A lesson mixes in the child's own past mistakes from the class sites, and notices when the same kind of mistake comes back.

Passing and trying again

A lesson passes at 75%; math-facts lessons at 90%. A retry teaches the same way again with new numbers or new stories.

The order of lessons

Lessons go in order. Math and reading take turns. Some strands wait until an earlier strand is passed.

Everything is read aloud

Every question, choice and story is read aloud, and must be heard to the end before the child can go on.

Records

Every try is saved to the results sheet; the teacher's dashboard shows each child's progress, the mistakes they repeat, and who is stuck.

Privacy

No student's name is written in the Lab or in this site. Who is on which plan comes from the results sheet; each child's evidence stays on the teacher's computer.
